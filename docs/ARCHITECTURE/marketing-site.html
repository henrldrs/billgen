§M00

Marketing UI/UX

The public pre-sale surface at billgen.be. A separate repository, a separate design layer, and a separate audience from the app this document sits beside — written 2026-09-02.

The marketing site is not the app. It keeps its own shadcn component layer rather than depending on @henrioutai/ui across repositories; what the two share is the brand — emerald #10B981, ink #1F2937, and Satoshi as the canonical face, mapped onto shadcn variables in globals.css.

Where it lives

Repo henrldrs/pre_sale_billgen, public. Local at ../billgen_presale_website.

Stack

Next.js 16.1.6, Tailwind v4, shadcn. Eight static pages, two dynamic route handlers, one proxy.

Deploys

Manual, npx vercel --prod, uploading the working copy rather than main. Connecting Vercel to GitHub is still open.

The page’s governing rule. Everything stated on the site must be something the product does today. BillGen exports Helger-validated Peppol BIS 3.0; it does not transmit over the Peppol network, and no wording in any of the four languages may imply otherwise — including wording lifted from a design mock.

§M01

Routes & Locales

Four languages as real URLs, not a client-side toggle — so each has a canonical address and search engines can be told they are the same page.

RouteKindNotes
/Proxy redirect307 on the billgen_locale cookie, then Accept-Language, then English. Vary: Cookie, Accept-Language.
/en /fr /nl /ptStaticThe page itself. generateStaticParams, dynamicParams = false.
/[locale]/privacyStaticFour more. Short, because the site genuinely does almost nothing.
/api/waitlistDynamicRegion fra1. Email capture.
/api/inquiryDynamicRegion fra1. Free-text requests with structured fields.

Locale plumbing

No i18n library. At roughly sixty strings across one page, next-intl would buy key lookup and locale routing that a typed object and a [locale] segment already give, at the cost of a dependency and a middleware config.

English is the source of truth in src/lib/content/en.ts and is declared as const; Dictionary is derived from it with a mapped type that widens string literals but keeps tuple lengths. A translation that drops a key, misspells one, ships eleven feature cards instead of twelve, or gives three role options instead of four fails the build. With four languages that guarantee is the only thing keeping the set honest.

Hreflang covers en-BE, fr-BE, nl-BE and pt-PT, with x-default on English. Portuguese is not pinned to Belgium so the page stays reachable from Portugal if the market ever widens.

Page Structure

Three acts: light, dark, light. The middle act is the product.

#whyLight, mutedCompliance is the floor, not the feature.

The dark band is a deliberate correction. The page previously ran twelve identically weighted white cards in a uniform grid, which tells a reader that twelve capabilities matter equally. They do not. Giving the product section its own ground separates it from the argument around it and gives the page a shape it did not have.

§M03

Components

Nine of them, all local to this repository.

SiteHeader

Sticky, translucent, backdrop-blurred. Wordmark, four section anchors, the locale control, and a pill CTA. On mobile the CTA is dropped rather than the language switcher — in a four-language market the toggle is the thing that must not be buried.

LanguageSwitcher

Segmented EN/FR/NL/PT built from real <Link>s, so a crawler without JavaScript can still walk between locales. Writes the cookie on click.

Hero

Badge, headline, subhead, CTA, reassurance line. No image — there is still no product screenshot worth showing.

GlassFilters / GlassCard

The frosted pane and the SVG filters it references. See Frosted Glass.

WaitlistForm

Email plus an off-screen honeypot. Posts to /api/waitlist; falls back to mailto: only when the endpoint reports storage is unconfigured.

InquiryForm

Name, email, company, market, role, message. Native select and textarea rather than Radix — three dependencies to render four options is not a trade worth making.

SiteFooter

Brand column with the closing pitch and its own CTA, then Product / Contact / Language columns, an oversized wordmark, and the cookie disclosure.

Privacy page

Six short sections. Shares the header and footer.

§M04

Visual Language

Deliberately unlike the four Belgian competitors, all of which are warm, photographic and consumer-adjacent.

The direction is a precise financial document, not another friendly SaaS landing page. Billit runs staged photography of tradespeople in branded orange; Dexxter runs saturated blue and yellow with floating UI fragments; Accountable reads as a consumer app; Moneybird is calm conventional blue. Building a fifth of those is parity. Reading like a ledger is not.

Ground

White and muted slate for the argument. A dark ink ledger field — #10131f to #171c2e — for the product.

Accent

Emerald, kept for actions, validation and active state. Not decoration.

Type

Satoshi, canonical per BRAND_TOKENS. Geist as the fallback stack.

§M05

Frosted Glass

From themed/billgen-frosted-glass-hero.html. The technique, and the one build trap in it.

An SVG turbulence and displacement filter stacked with a real blur and a saturation boost, plus an inset top highlight that sells the edge of the pane:

  • feTurbulence fractal noise, blurred, driving an feDisplacementMap
  • backdrop-filter: url(#glass-distortion) blur(6px) saturate(160%)
  • Inset highlight and hairline, plus a deep drop shadow
  • A -webkit- fallback of plain blur, because Safari ignores url() references inside backdrop-filter

Two things the technique needs, and one that breaks it.

It needs a ground worth distorting. Frosted glass on white over white is invisible, which is why the sections that use it carry their own dark ledger backdrop with rule lines rather than sitting on the page white.

It breaks in the build. Lightning CSS — the optimiser Tailwind v4 runs inside the Next build — deletes backdrop-filter: url(#id) … from the compiled stylesheet, treating the SVG filter reference as unsupported by a build target. The declaration never reaches a browser that does in fact support it. The filter is therefore set as an inline style in components/glass.tsx, which is not put through that pass. If the frost ever disappears after a refactor, this is the first thing to check.

The source mock also carried the line “Facture envoyée via Peppol — statut : acceptée par le destinataire.” That copy was not used: it claims network transmission the product does not do.

§M06

Capture & Data

Two channels, one storage rule: write it down first, tell someone second.

The original plan had the route send an email and treat that as the record, which makes a failed send a lost prospect. The order is inverted: validate, throttle, store, then attempt a best-effort notification whose failure is swallowed.

ChannelCapturesKey
waitlist email, locale, timestamp Hash by address — signing up twice is idempotent, and a deletion request is one HDEL.
inquiries name, email, company, role, market, message, locale, timestamp Hash by timestamp and address — the same person may legitimately write twice about two different things.

Why role and market are fields

So the answers are countable. One prospect asking whether BillGen can do Netherlands invoicing is an anecdote; twenty of them is a roadmap decision, and that only becomes visible if the market is a column rather than a sentence buried in free text.

Defences

  • Off-screen honeypot on both forms — display:none is the first thing a bot checks for
  • Per-IP counter expiring after an hour; five waitlist attempts, three inquiries
  • Server-side length caps and a permissive email shape check
  • A broken throttle fails open — losing a real prospect is worse than accepting one extra

Storage is Upstash Redis over REST, region Frankfurt. Both routes answer 503 unconfigured until the credentials exist, which is what keeps the mailto: fallback honest.

§M07

Privacy Posture

Short, because the site genuinely does almost nothing — and that is a selling point for a compliance product.

  • One cookie. billgen_locale, set only on an explicit click, holding a two-letter code. Functional under the ePrivacy rules, so no consent banner — but disclosed in the footer and on the privacy page, because the information duty remains.
  • No analytics, no third-party scripts, no profiling. The footer says so in four languages. If a tracker is ever added, that sentence changes first.
  • EU storage. Frankfurt, with the receiving function pinned to the same region.
  • Deletion on request, without asking why. One HDEL.

Every competitor in the benchmark opens with a cookie consent dialog. Not needing one is worth saying out loud.

§M08

Positioning

Rewritten 2026-09-02 after benchmarking Billit, Dexxter, Accountable and Moneybird.

Peppol is commoditised risk

Accountable gives it away in a badge above its H1. Dexxter advertises it unlimited. Billit is an access point with a free capped tier. The page no longer leads with it.

The fiduciaire portal is occupied risk

Billit and Dexxter both run free accountant portals, Billit exporting UBL into thirty-plus Belgian packages. Multi-tenancy alone is not a differentiator.

Integrity is unclaimed own it

Four competitors sell time and calm. None sells provable correctness. Gapless numbering, nothing hard-deleted, immutable sent documents, validation before send.

The headline follows from that: correct before it leaves, provable after. It changes who the page attracts — accountants and audit-conscious businesses rather than freelancers shopping on price — and it is the one sentence no competitor in the set can copy.

Desktop mode is the second unclaimed asset: all four competitors are cloud-only, and none can offer to keep a client’s books on the practice’s own machine.
